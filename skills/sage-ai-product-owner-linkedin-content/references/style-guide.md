# Style guide

These rules are the skill's defaults. The Product Owner's content profile can change a rule where it says so, with a reason. The voice of a post always comes from the Product Owner's words in the transcript.

## Voice

- Write in the first person, as the Product Owner. Use the transcript to learn their word choices, jargon, phrasing, and habits of speech. Match them. Clean them up and polish them a little. Do not make them sound like someone else.
- The interviewer has no voice. A question or prompt from the interviewer is a scaffold for a topic. Added nuance or perspective from the interviewer counts as the Product Owner's input. The interviewer's name and the word interviewer never appear in a post.
- Be clear, professional, concise, and engaging. Keep it business lighthearted. Self-deprecating humor works.
- Write for emotional resonance. Tell relatable stories. Include the meaningful details. A post may run longer than its template implies, and the batch should mix short and long posts.
- Optimize for LinkedIn impressions, using what is known about the algorithm.

## Sentences

- Write at an eighth grade reading level.
- Keep every sentence under 10 words. Use short, punchy sentences. A sentence of 10 words or more fails the validator.
- A list line counts as a sentence. Keep it under 10 words too.
- Put a blank line between paragraphs, so the post is easy to skim. Paragraphs are one or two short sentences.
- A few template lines are fixed and stay as written, even where they run long. The validator knows them. See templates.md.

## Characters

- Never use an em dash or an en dash, in any field. Use separate short sentences.
- Never use emoticons or emoji.
- Use symbols sparingly. Use only the characters in the library below.
- Start every list line with one character from the library. Do not use periods after list items, because they may or may not be complete sentences. If a list item holds more than one complete sentence, use periods.
- Do not use a hyphen, a number, or a dot as a list marker.

## Engagement

Do not write engagement farming questions or calls to action. This includes What is your experience, Let me know in the comments, What do you think, and anything like them. Let the content speak for itself. A fixed template line that asks a question stays, because the template defines it. Do not add a closing question of your own.

## Hooks

Every template has three hook slots. Hook 1 opens the body. Hooks 2 and 3 are alternates and go in the notes. Make each hook attention getting, scroll stopping, and curious. Each includes the main ideas of the topic. The three hooks differ in angle and not only in wording.

## Image text and share text

Image text is copy for the image card. It works as an advertisement for the post.

- Headline and subtext is the common form. The headline is bold, with 5 to 8 words. The subtext supports it and creates curiosity, with 5 to 8 words.
- Headline and points is for list posts. The headline is a bold statement about the list. Add 2 or 3 points of 3 to 5 words each.
- Keep the text minimal. Make the headline contrarian, intriguing, or bold. Create a curiosity gap. A pull quote from the post sometimes works.

Share text is one or two sentences used when the post is shared. It teases the content without giving away the insight, the lesson, or the punchline. It must make sense on its own. It can pose a question or make a bold statement.

The three horizontal lines (―, ─, ═) are in the library but look like dashes. Do not use them as a dash. The validator treats them as allowed. A list line starts with any library character except those three.

Typographic quotes and apostrophes are allowed in text. Letters with accents are allowed. Any other symbol fails the validator.
